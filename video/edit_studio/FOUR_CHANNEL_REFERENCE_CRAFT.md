# Reference-led premium edits — four-channel research and craft standard
**Updated: 2026-11-04. Applies to NEW universal-edit schema v2 projects only.**

## Evidence limits (important)
The supplied images are **YouTube channel ABOUT screenshots**, not actual video frames. Public source listings, tutorials and a published dialogue transcript were examined, but direct playback/frame-by-frame inspection of these exact channels was unavailable during this research. **No frame durations, cuts, color values, whooshes, or transition positions below are claimed to have been measured from their videos.** Before any production can score as reference-matched, a reviewer must play at least one actual reference **with audio**, record three timestamped observations and compare the final MP4. The reference_quality_gate.py enforces this at *release*.

## The easily missed craft choices we require

1. **The first visual must mean something.** No title card, generic camera push or slow greeting. Viewer gets a legible face/action/question by <=1.5s. Keep a distinct stop-scroll frame.
2. **Build one authored idea, not a scene montage.** Use hook → setup/tension → turn → payoff; comic edits use setup → misdirection → punchline. Every new clip must change the viewer's understanding, intensity or emotional expectation.
3. **Keep complete micro-actions.** Preserve the setup motion AND reaction: body turn, eye movement, punch contact, shot hitting net, character smile, reveal or dialogue response. Trim only material that does not affect understanding.
4. **Edit by meaningful action boundaries, not equal clip lengths.** Pause before the reveal when needed. Faster is not always better. Preserve reaction room after the musical accent instead of cutting out on the peak.
5. **Eye-trace continuity.** In vertical reframing, track the important face/object/ball and keep successive shots visually related. Avoid abrupt left-right jumps unless that contrast is intentional. Check foreground/background separations at phone size.
6. **Motion direction + speed-ramp handles.** Use ramps *only where the source action justifies a change* and check adjacent frames for warping, duplicated motion and stutter. Ease into and out of the peak; retain normal-speed anchors for the crucial physical contact/reaction. Twixtor is a technique named in a creator tutorial, not a required paid plugin.
7. **Cut on a reason.** Candidate cut motivations: a completed gesture, matching gaze, camera-movement direction, shift of speaker, realization, score/payoff, rhythmic accent. Describe which one justifies each cut.
8. **Transitions should be nearly invisible unless narrative demands a reveal.** A straight cut is preferred by default. Masking must accurately track occlusions. Whip, flash and shake require a real visual purpose; inspect for unwanted 1-frame black/white flashes and edge tearing.
9. **Subtle image finishing.** Match exposure, skin tone, black level, contrast and white balance across sources before stylization. Keep highlights and facial texture intact; never blindly sharpen/rescale heavily. Check dark scenes on a normal phone screen, not just desktop.
10. **Dialogue-first sound hierarchy.** Dialogue must be understandable above the licensed track. Existing scene sound/physical Foley can continue under adjacent cuts when rights permit; impacts must match actual contact. Use short natural fades where source cuts click. Never stack an unmotivated whoosh on every cut.
11. **Map music landmarks before assembly.** Mark entrance, tension rise, first drop, rhythmic accents, tail. Place events because they coincide with narrative turns; do not sacrifice a finished sentence or reaction to hit a grid.
12. **Let the soundtrack breathe.** Controlled pre-laps and post-laps can smoothly link legitimate sources. Avoid background-music sudden jumps, crushed dynamics, clipping and artificial silence. If a reference's music is not cleared, use a separately licensed replacement instead of extracting it.
13. **Typography is secondary.** Most frames should show the source subject. Keep only essential readable names/quotes; constrain outline and shadows; avoid clutter and overlays where YouTube/TikTok controls cover content. Use legitimately licensed fonts.
14. **No fake cinematic filler.** No AI pictures, synthetic character shots, AI voice/music, generated hands, unrelated archive, screensaver particles or pseudo-3D filler. Only real cleared source material and manually authored edits/graphics that genuinely explain something.
15. **Do not confuse technical polish with originality.** Original interpretation, visual explanation, comic recombination or critical structure must be clear. Effects, licensing and credit alone never make a reused clip monetization-safe.
16. **Compare outcomes.** Watch the user's rendered film muted, then at normal audio, then at phone size. Compare with an actually watched source reference *at equivalent viewing conditions*. Record specific observed deficiencies; never fabricate a review or inflate a subjective score.

## Practical timing guidance — design heuristics, NOT measurements of these channels
For a 20–35 second Short: put the first hook in 0–1.5s; let the setup breathe for about 2–7s; aim to connect turning point and first meaningful audio peak around 7–13s; allow an intense middle; conclude on a visible/audible payoff, with time for the expression or completed action. These are deliberately flexible and must yield to the real source.

For beat sync, inspect approximate hit alignment to within 2–3 frames at 30fps as an **editorial target**, not a guarantee that such timing was observed in the reference channels. If preserving a line or physical impact makes exact sync inappropriate, preserve meaning and annotate the exception. Do not apply shake, zoom, mask, flash, vignette, grain and 3D text simultaneously by default.

## Fast free pipeline (no automatic final montage)
- Existing FFmpeg / ffprobe workflow: codec/resolution/level checks, precisely timed cuts, legal reframes, carefully authored motion, fades and audio mix. https://ffmpeg.org/
- **PySceneDetect** (BSD-3-Clause): index possible cut boundaries so an editor can inspect and choose the best action. https://github.com/Breakthrough/PySceneDetect
- **LosslessCut** (free and open source): quick **rough** trims from rights-cleared local masters; frame-exact finishing still done in final conform. https://github.com/mifi/lossless-cut
- **Auto-Editor** (Unlicense): optional silence/motion *suggestions* on a long owned source for finding candidates; never blindly remove a comedic pause or final human reaction. https://github.com/WyattBlue/auto-editor
- No paid plugin, external AI media-generation service or mobile-data-heavy file transfer is required for this policy check. Only fetch a reference/source if viewing or rights are legitimate. Maintain one local/Drive media cache to avoid redownloads.

## Enforced workflow for every NEW edit
1. Select story/punchline and **licensed** source(s); verify rights at the exact clip level.
2. Research at least 2 relevant reference URLs. Explicitly label whether evidence is metadata, transcript, tutorial or actual frame+audio inspection.
3. Before release, **personally watch/listen to at least 1 chosen reference** and note 3+ observed image/sound timestamps. Do not mislabel a thumbnail or transcript as watched video.
4. Write the **schema_version: 2** universal edit manifest. Fill reference_profile: story intent, beat_map, each shot's eye-trace, cut motivation, audio/typing notes, no AI assets and source evidence. Preserve the existing universal rights/originality fields.
5. Run `python3 video/edit_studio/release_gate.py --manifest FILE --phase plan`. This evaluates reference craft and original universal gates; a rights risk may be catalogued for research but cannot pass release.
6. Make the human-authored edit using the actual source media and cleared music/SFX. Do not use an AI-generated insert to hide missing real footage.
7. Watch and listen to the actual export, compare to a *watched reference*, check safe areas and flash/click artifacts. Give **independently judged** scores 0–10 on hook, payoff, shot selection, rhythm, motivated motion/effects, color/subject clarity, dialogue/music/foley, mobile readability, and original contribution. **Every category must be >=8.5**. If it is below, **HOLD**; correct and rewatch. Metadata/tool assertions are not a substitute.
8. Sign the exact export SHA256 in both editorial_review and reference_profile.human_reference_comparison; run the universal **release** gate. The special football, history or other stricter rights gates also remain.
9. No auto-publication; deliver actual verified media and credits via Google Drive when specifically asked for a video, not by making up a Drive URL.

The v1 schema remains readable for old manifests. **No new production should start at v1.** This addition is a configuration change, not a request to render a video.
