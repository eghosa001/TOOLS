## 1. Boston documentary

**Recommended title:** Boston's Deadly Molasses Flood: The Warnings Nobody Acted On

**Alternate titles:** Why 2.3 Million Gallons of Molasses Flooded Boston in 1919 · The Molasses Flood Wasn't a Freak Accident — Here's What Failed

**Description:**

On January 15, 1919, a storage tank ruptured in Boston's North End, releasing roughly 2.3 million gallons of molasses. Twenty-one people died and about 150 were injured. But the warning signs had been visible before the disaster.

This documentary reconstructs the sequence of events using official Boston and Massachusetts historical records, surviving archival photographs, and clear engineering explanations. We examine the leaking tank, the damage to the elevated railway, the desperate rescue, and the legal case that followed.

## 2. Herculaneum documentary

**Recommended title:** Scientists Read a Roman Scroll Without Opening It — What Was Inside?

**Alternate titles:** The Roman Library We Can Finally Read: Herculaneum's 2026 Breakthrough · How Scientists Unwrapped a 2,000-Year-Old Scroll Without Touching It

**Description:**

In June 2026, researchers announced that they had virtually unwrapped and read the surviving portion of a Herculaneum papyrus known as PHerc. 1667 — without physically opening it.

We explore how X-ray imaging, surface reconstruction, machine learning and expert papyrologists recovered writing from a library buried by Mount Vesuvius in AD 79. We also explain two *different* findings: PHerc. 172, associated with Philodemus's On Vices, Book One, and PHerc. 139, identified as On Gods, Book Eight. Those discoveries must not be confused with PHerc. 1667.

The film distinguishes confirmed science from remaining uncertainties and labels illustrations rather than presenting them as original ancient images.
