## Short 01 — Titanic's lifeboat calculation
Target 45–60 sec **after audio timing**. Original narration:

> Titanic carried twenty lifeboats. According to the British inquiry, they had room for eleven hundred and seventy-eight people. But that same report counted two thousand two hundred and one passengers and crew aboard. Even with every place filled, more than a thousand people would have been left without a seat. Some lifeboats also launched partly empty. Many passengers struggled to understand that the ship was sinking; crews faced a shrinking window to evacuate people into small boats over a freezing sea. The tragedy was not simply a collision with ice. It exposed a rescue system that could never evacuate everyone aboard. Our full documentary examines the evidence behind those final hours.

Visual sequence (vertical composition, safe margins):
1. Narration "twenty lifeboats" → original **20-boat deck schematic**, 14/2/4 distinct.
2. "1,178" → large capacity figure with British report citation, restrained sound bed.
3. "2,201" → overlay count against capacity as **factual numerical comparison**.
4. "more than a thousand" → empty-seats graphic; do **not** imply all 1,178 seats could physically have been filled.
5. "Some lifeboats also launched partly empty" → authentic 1912 survivors' lifeboat photo, **rescue context caption**, not a staged launching photo: https://www.loc.gov/item/2014691299/; rights check pending.
6. "shrinking window" → accurate time span 11:40–2:20, ship time.
7. Final CTA → branded original typographic end frame.

Citations: https://www.titanicinquiry.org/BOTInq/BOTReport/BOTRepBoats.php ; https://www.titanicinquiry.org/BOTApp/BOTApp10.php ; https://blogs.loc.gov/law/2012/04/failure-to-update-the-law-a-titanic-mistake/ . ## Short 02 — Titanic's wreck is changing
Target 45–60 sec **after audio timing**. Original narration:

> More than a century after Titanic sank, the wreck is still changing. During a twenty twenty-four expedition, investigators found that a fifteen-foot section of railing had fallen from the port side of the bow. The same expedition rediscovered a bronze statue called Diana of Versailles in the debris field. Separately, researchers surveyed the wreck in twenty twenty-two, building a remarkable digital record showcased in twenty twenty-five. These discoveries do not answer every question about the final night. They help historians compare old testimony with physical evidence. As more of Titanic deteriorates, documenting what remains becomes increasingly important. Our full documentary follows the evidence.
