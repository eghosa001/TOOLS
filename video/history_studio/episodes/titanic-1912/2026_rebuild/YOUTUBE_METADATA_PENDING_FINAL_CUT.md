## Main description
On the night of 14 April 1912, RMS Titanic struck an iceberg in the North Atlantic. About two hours and forty minutes later, the ship was gone. The story of what happened in between is more complex—and more human—than the familiar image of an iceberg and an unsinkable ship.

In this evidence-led documentary from Facts Uncovered: Stories & Evidence, we trace Titanic's construction and first voyage, the ice warnings, the collision, the flooding, the lifeboats, the wireless distress calls, the Carpathia rescue, and the investigations that followed. We also explore what the discovery of the wreck in 1985, subsequent underwater expeditions, and newer mapping efforts have added to the historical record.

Survivors sometimes disagreed, official totals differed, and some modern explanations remain disputed. We distinguish the original testimony from later interpretation and explicitly label reconstructions.

Subscribe for carefully researched documentaries about history, mysteries, discoveries and the evidence behind them.
