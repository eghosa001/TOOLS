# Satisfying Texture ASMR: YouTube SEO research and operating playbook

**Prepared:** 2 November 2026  
**Scope:** Search and recommendations strategy for the Satisfying Texture ASMR channel. This playbook does not modify any published video or channel settings.  
**Evidence basis:** Current official YouTube Help and Culture & Trends sources, plus the repository's existing ASMR production and editorial records. Keyword popularity is not represented as measured unless YouTube Studio supplies it.

## Executive findings

YouTube SEO is a viewer-fit and satisfaction discipline. Search considers relevance, engagement and quality; recommendations are personalized and consider whether viewers choose to watch, keep watching, and enjoy a video. Topic interest, competition and seasonality also change reach.

The most important video-level levers are the actual experience, title, thumbnail, description and alignment between them. Tags are low-priority and mainly useful for common misspellings. For Shorts, measure `Shown in feed`, `How many chose to view` / `Stayed to watch`, engaged views and average percentage viewed. For long-form, use impressions, CTR, search terms, average view duration, average percentage viewed, retention and traffic sources. Never blend the two formats into one baseline.

Because views are currently arriving, leave existing metadata alone until the right ASMR channel connection and per-video baseline are available. YouTube warns that title and thumbnail changes can change viewer response and advises against changing what is working. This is a risk-control choice, not a promise that any edit can preserve views.

## Research findings

### Search and recommendation systems

YouTube Search describes three broad elements: relevance, engagement and quality. Relevance may consider how the title, tags, description and video content match the query. Engagement includes watch time for a query. Search results are not simply sorted by total views.

Recommendations personalize around viewers. YouTube describes signals including what an individual watches, what videos are watched together, how much of a topic or channel a viewer watches, whether a recommendation is selected or ignored, and whether viewers stay. Average view duration and average percentage viewed are named signals. Reach also depends on topic interest, competition and seasonality. There is no defensible universal CTR, retention, upload-time or title-length threshold that guarantees growth.

**Channel implication:** metadata can accurately label the sensory experience, but cannot compensate for weak opening seconds, mismatched sound, misleading packaging or content that fails its promise. Keep the channel promise coherent: original tactile visuals, satisfying transformations and close matched sound. Say “no talking,” “no music,” “3D,” or “real recorded sound” only when true of the exact upload.

### Titles and thumbnails

YouTube recommends accurate, succinct titles, placing important words near the beginning and avoiding misleading or sensational wording. Choose searchable or intriguing packaging based on where the likely audience might find the video. Thumbnails should accurately represent the content. Eligible public long-form videos can use YouTube Studio title/thumbnail A/B testing, with watch time used to choose a winner; Shorts are not eligible for that test. Shorts can use a selected frame as a thumbnail in supported upload flows.

**Guideline, not a rigid template:**

`[Visible material/action] ASMR | [specific tactile payoff or honest viewing condition]`

Examples for future uploads only:

- `Glossy Candy Shell Cracking ASMR | Five Fruit Textures`
- `Glass Marbles Through a Crystal Spiral | 3D ASMR`
- `Falling Rain on Glass and Leaves | Relaxing ASMR`

Use searchable packaging for a recognizable object/action (`glass marble run`, `rain on window`, `crunchy fruit ASMR`). A curiosity-led phrase is appropriate only when the visuals immediately prove it. Keep the actual action in front; do not let generic repeated prefixes crowd it out.

Thumbnail/frame checklist: one readable object or contact moment; strong phone-size contrast; action visible, not only a pretty still; no implied event the video does not fulfill; no competitor imagery. For Shorts, select a strong frame from the actual master. Do not replace a thumbnail on an active video without a deliberate test or clear evidence.

### Descriptions, tags and hashtags

Descriptions have a 5,000-character ceiling. YouTube recommends unique descriptions, using the first lines to explain the video, and featuring one or two main words prominently in both title and description. Studio search terms reveal what viewers actually typed. Chapters help longer videos when timestamps are real and useful. Related playlists can help viewers find a coherent series.

Tags play a minimal role except for common misspellings. Excessive tags in descriptions violate YouTube spam guidance. Hashtags are navigational labels; use a few relevant ones, not a block of unrelated trends.

**Description structure:**

1. Two accurate viewer-facing lines: action/material plus sound/format.
2. One short detail sentence describing sequence and original 3D/physical method, only if confirmed.
3. Relevant series/playlist link or credits, when applicable.
4. Up to three focused hashtags at the end.

Avoid keyword paragraphs, boilerplate copied to every upload, invented chapters, provenance claims not supported by the ledger, or false “no talking/no music” labels.

### Shorts-specific signals and format

YouTube Studio's Shorts reports distinguish raw views from engaged viewing behavior. Track chosen-to-view / stayed-to-watch and average percentage viewed alongside engaged views, retention and subscriber change. Shorts thumbnails matter in places such as channel shelves and search; the Shorts feed depends on swipe/view response and subsequent satisfaction. Long-form thumbnail CTR benchmarks do not apply to Shorts.

For ASMR, start with the tactile event or clear anticipation; remove unrelated title cards; complete a satisfying gesture; end on a resolved image/sound or purposeful loop. Keep action close and contact audible in the opening. Evaluate length against comparable uploads on this channel instead of imitating unrelated viral Shorts.

### Channel identity, series and playlists

`Satisfying Texture ASMR` clearly communicates category and sensory intent. Keep a consistent visual identity while allowing material and action to vary. Useful playlist ideas include `Glass & Marble ASMR`, `Crunchy Shell ASMR`, `Rain & Water ASMR`, and `Long Relaxing ASMR`. Do not reorganize the homepage or rename playlists during active growth without checking channel traffic and playlist behavior.

Channel keywords, upload defaults and boilerplate are not substitutes for video-level relevance. Upload defaults only apply to browser uploads, not necessarily API or external-tool uploads. Keep defaults generic and add per-video phrases only after the exact upload is known.

### Keyword research: evidence and limits

The connected YouTube Data API in `video/youtube_research_cloud` performs public competitor search and reports observed public views/upload dates for a relevance-selected sample. Its documentation correctly says this is not search volume, keyword difficulty, current velocity or representative niche demand. The repo's ASMR workflow uses its configured YouTube API integration.

Use this free evidence hierarchy:

1. YouTube Studio `YouTube Search` terms for the actual ASMR videos/channel.
2. YouTube Studio Research tab and viewer search data, when available for the owner channel.
3. Google Trends as relative interest over time/geography, not absolute YouTube volume.
4. Public YouTube search and competitor examples as qualitative packaging evidence, not market-size estimates.
5. Label each phrase `observed`, `derived`, or `untested`.

Starter phrase clusters for future uploads (editorial seeds, **not** volume-ranked):

- `ASMR`, `satisfying ASMR`, `oddly satisfying`
- Material + action: `glass marble ASMR`, `marble run`, `crunchy shell ASMR`, `fruit cutting ASMR`, `rain sounds ASMR`, `glass sounds`
- Format/intent: `no talking ASMR`, `3D ASMR`, `relaxing sounds`, `Short ASMR`, `long ASMR`

Use each phrase only if the upload matches it. Avoid broad unrelated phrases solely because they appear popular.

YouTube Culture & Trends describes oddly satisfying viewing across kinetic sand, slime, colorful objects and craftsmanship, and later highlighted surreal/impossible-material ASMR. These reports show category breadth, not a guarantee that an upload will trend. The defensible editorial position is original, clearly labeled tactile fantasy/CGI ASMR with action-matched sound. Production quality and a clear sensory payoff lead; trend phrases are secondary.

## Safe application

### Existing uploads: protect active performance

- Do not rename, retitle, rewrite descriptions, replace thumbnails, edit tags, change privacy, delete/re-upload or change channel settings while the channel is receiving views.
- First capture a baseline: video IDs, current metadata, publication date, privacy, lifetime and last-7/28-day views, impressions, CTR (long-form), Shorts shown-in-feed/chose-to-view, engaged views, average percentage viewed, search terms and traffic sources.
- Preserve an immutable before-snapshot. Confirm the connected identity is the ASMR owner channel and use read-only access for the audit.
- Avoid edits to accelerating or healthy uploads. If an actual metadata mismatch emerges, prepare one exact proposed field change and obtain owner approval. Change one field at a time and observe at least seven days. This does not guarantee views will hold.
- The repo's configured YouTube API path supports public research; keep it separate from owner-channel OAuth. Never put API keys, refresh tokens or access tokens in client code, Git, logs or reports.

### New uploads: pre-publish workflow

1. Confirm the exact channel and final MP4; complete the ASMR release review first. SEO is not publication approval.
2. Write three accurate title candidates: searchable, sensory/payoff-led and hybrid. Keep each below the 100-character limit.
3. Select the title that makes the viewer promise clearest. Create a truthful thumbnail or select a strong Shorts frame from the final master.
4. Draft a unique description with the target action/material in the opening lines. Confirm every claim against the approved asset and audio ledger.
5. Add only a small set of accurate tags if useful for misspellings or disambiguation. Use a few relevant hashtags, if any.
6. Select a coherent playlist; link a related video only if it exists and is relevant.
7. Keep visibility and scheduling settings as explicitly authorized. Never auto-publish.
8. Record final title, description, thumbnail/frame, tags, date and media hash in a metadata ledger.

### Measurement and diagnosis

Review at 24–48 hours for delivery or technical problems, but do not make knee-jerk edits from tiny samples. At 7 and 28 days compare each upload against the channel's median for the same format and similar age. Use at least three comparable uploads before adopting a channel-level rule. Separate Shorts from long-form, series, ages and acquisition surfaces. Test one packaging variable at a time; native YouTube A/B testing is preferable to manual sequential edits on an actively viewed video.

| Symptom | Inspect first | Low-risk response |
|---|---|---|
| Shorts shown in feed but low chose-to-view | First frame, immediate action, title promise | Improve the next Short's opening; leave the live one untouched unless it is clearly misleading |
| Strong chose-to-view but weak average percentage viewed | Pacing, payoff timing, sound/action continuity | Improve the next edit; do not change unrelated metadata |
| Long-form impressions but weak CTR | Traffic surface, title/thumbnail coherence, sample size | Consider native Studio A/B testing only if eligible and adequately sampled |
| Good CTR but weak retention | Expectation mismatch, slow opening, sound quality | Fix the next production's promise/structure; title changes alone can worsen mismatch |
| Search traffic exists but query relevance is poor | Studio search terms vs title/description/video | Make future metadata more precise; edit a live field only if mismatch is material and confirmed |
| Reach falls while engagement is stable | Topic interest, competition, seasonality, traffic source mix | Do not assume a metadata penalty; compare equivalent periods and sources |
