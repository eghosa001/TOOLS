## Deficiencies found in our previous instructions and implementation

1. **No CGI-only peer benchmark.** The 11-source archive is valuable for sound/process/surreal editing, but it cannot substitute for actual physics/rendering comparisons with demonstrably original Blender CGI. For any new CGI format, obtain one applicable reference and record full independent video-plus-headphone review, with source URL, timecoded observations, display size, realistic material response, auditory dynamics and viewing method. Titles/descriptions cannot grant approval.
2. **Incomplete material taxonomy.** Elastic and fluid solvers are documented; **granular sand, fracture/tearing, adhesive/viscoplastic pulling, and shell-to-interior transitions** were not explicitly classified as their own simulation needs. Select real collision and fragment/particle/cohesion behaviour; document topology and release.
3. **Temporal CGI stability needs its own acceptance evidence.** Critically review consecutive frames through first contact, maximum stress, detachment and rest. Block mesh clipping, jitter, volume popping, texture swimming, refraction/denoise flicker, missed impacts, unnatural repeated frames or discontinuous speed. A full video decode or generic 'motion 9/10' alone does not prove this.
4. **Output profile was hard-coded 9:16 despite a 16:9 long-form editorial rule.** The main quality gate now supports explicit `delivery_aspect_ratio: "16:9"` (legacy Shorts stay default `9:16`). Long-film plans also need `production_kind: "long-form"` for >180s and <=2400s; the 180s single-material episode builder remains a **specialist**, not an eight-minute wide builder.
5. **No calibrated motion-quality/export strategy against peers.** Creator-described 4K60 can make 720p24 visibly different, especially for rapid particle/cloth/rigid-body contact. **Do not mandate 4K60 on limited hardware** or upscale 720 and call it native 4K. Use deliberate 24/30/60 fps based on movement and render budget, preview cadence, preserve native detail, and verify actual exported frame cadence, bitrate artifacts, and motion readability on phone plus full screen. For strong wide 3D showcase consider native 1920×1080+ if computationally feasible; native 1280×720 is the release floor under the existing user-approved minimum.
6. **Shot-to-shot sound perspective.** Beyond matching action/force, the Foley should reflect apparent distance, contact size, damping, surface resonance, stereo position and room acoustics. Avoid the same close, sharp sample for near and far objects; test both normal-volume headphones and phone. No fabricated stereo panning to hide a mismatch.
7. **No mandatory side-by-side 3D peer acceptance sheet.** Compare an approved still, two key contact/release frame sequences, and sound at comparable normalised playback levels, not only a scripted nine-dimensional score. Log where the peer is stronger and what was actually modified before marking the export ready.
