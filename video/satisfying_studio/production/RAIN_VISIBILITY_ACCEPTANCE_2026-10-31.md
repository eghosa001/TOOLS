# Rain ASMR falling-rain visibility acceptance standard
Last updated: 2026-10-31

A sequence of wet surfaces, raindrops already stuck to glass, stationary backgrounds, roof edges, wet leaves, ripples, or bokeh does **not** by itself qualify as a falling-rain ASMR video. A technically valid 2–3-minute edit can still fail the core visual premise.

## Gate before any full render
1. Review 6–15 seconds **as a moving video at 360p**, not a frame contact sheet only. The rain must be obviously falling at ordinary mobile playback size without pausing or zooming.
2. In the first 2 seconds, show visible rain actively traveling from top to bottom or at an angle: at least several ongoing raindrop streams or streaks with visible per-frame displacement.
3. Rain should remain visible across the main body. Reject a scene where the only movement is leaf flutter, a rivulet fixed on glass, driving traffic, wet ground, or changing reflections.
4. For a 2:45–2:55 Short, vary genuine licensed moving footage and use clean, visually attractive scenes. Do not stretch a still photograph or repeat a 6-second shot dozens of times.
5. If a procedural rain effect augments camera footage, identify it as an editorial effect in the source ledger; do not describe it as completely camera-captured rain. Keep motion downward, randomized, nonlooping, perspective-aware and sufficiently visible in the compressed low-data version.
6. Retain actual recorded continuous rain sound. No unrelated effects; loudness, silence and peak gates remain required. Do not assert frame-accurate droplet Foley from continuous ambience.
7. Save a mobile-resolution first 20-second preview and at least one before/after motion check; do not render or upload a full master until the rain visibility gate passes.
8. Review audio and video end to end by a human before labeling as premium/publication-approved. Never auto-publish an unapproved visual correction.
