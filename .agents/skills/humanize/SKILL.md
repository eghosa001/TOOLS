# Humanize — Spoken Narration

**Purpose:** Make audio scripts sound like a thoughtful human storyteller speaking naturally to a listener. Apply while drafting, not as a cosmetic rewrite afterwards. Preserve source accuracy and the intended meaning.

## Write for the ear

1. Open with a concrete, compelling fact, action, mystery or question that the episode actually answers. Make the listener care without manufacturing drama.
2. Give the listener one clear idea at a time. Prefer familiar, precise words and conversational syntax. Vary sentence length and rhythm so the narration does not become monotonous.
3. Organize the story as an understandable sequence of events, causes, consequences and revelations. Use names and dates only when they help the listener follow the story; reintroduce people or places naturally after long gaps.
4. Use natural transitions and specific details rather than generic filler. Treat uncertainty honestly; separate established facts from disputed accounts and possibilities.
5. Write an ending that resolves the central question or leaves one genuinely meaningful thought. Avoid automatic moral lessons or unnecessary calls to action.
6. For Shorts, make the first spoken line work alone, establish the point quickly, and make the payoff intelligible without depending on the long video.

## Voice and TTS

- Scripts are **words to be spoken**, not articles copied into narration. No Markdown headings, citations, stage directions, URLs, tables or parenthetical production notes in the spoken text.
- Keep voice directions, scene cues, source citations and pronunciation guidance in **separate** fields or companion notes compatible with the existing 2D episode manifest.
- Write numbers, acronyms, foreign names and unfamiliar terms so a voice actor or TTS system can read them naturally. Check intended pronunciations rather than inventing them.
- Leave room for deliberate pacing, emotion and silence where the story merits it. Do not stuff the narration with artificial exclamation marks or ellipses.
- Respect the actual audio/runtime target; do not pad with repetition to reach a video duration. Keep character dialogue distinct from narrator voice.

## Editorial pass before delivery

Read the script aloud mentally from start to finish: does it sound like a person explaining an interesting true story? Remove formulaic AI phrases, inflated adjectives, repeated hooks, artificial contrasts, mechanical three-part lists, lectures, vague statements and unnecessary restatement. Tighten every sentence that doesn't move the story forward.

Check every historical claim against the research. Preserve citations in production notes. Never invent eyewitness quotes, archival details, factual certainty, views, searches or audience reactions. Do not change a claim merely to make it more exciting.

## Hand-off

Deliver the finalized spoken narration and separately structured citations, pronunciation notes and scene metadata required by `video/history_studio/2d/`. When asked for audio, pass the approved words into the existing narration pipeline; this skill changes writing quality, **not** the TTS voice engine or audio file itself.
